# Before the Name — submission spine v1

2026-11-01 · Speaker: Mark (MarkDude) · Editorial lead: Meraki

**Through-line:** offer a work, distinguish the questions it raises, and let an informed encounter remain open.

| Beat | What happens | Transition / anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. A small card | Offer an illustrative postcard before explaining the proposed group project. A viewer can decline or dislike it. | One encounter → a proposed group → the encounter again. |
| 2. The name is doing several jobs | Separate medium, subject/form, art-status, quality, taste and production. | A particular response → broader questions; “AI-generated” adds information. |
| 3. This may remain rubbish | Compare actual discarded material with an openly generated arrangement; selection does not transfer every contribution. | Labels → a concrete specimen → source and presentation choices. |
| 4. Monet, and a pond | Move from an established name to a human pond photograph, its genuine manual GIMP edit and an ordinary generated pond. Keep the latter's label visible. | Reputation and appearance → an actual process account. |
| 5. One picture I will claim | A shared-making photograph gives the speaker a particular artistic stake; another participant's contribution remains visible in the account. | General discussion → brief personal testimony → room for others' judgments. |
| 6. A credit is useful, and insufficient | Compare three fictional postcard proposals; give the strongest production objection its full weight; return to the same generated pond with source foregrounded. | Aesthetic selection → production questions → another encounter with the same image. |
| 7. Back to the small card | Return to a proposed encounter among makers, a venue and visitors. Standards, refusal and participation all matter. Support may help the next work happen. | Broader principles → a modest practical proposal. |
| 8. Leave the water some room | Return to the human pond photograph; stop explaining and leave the image. | Discussion → looking → end. |

## Proposed session shape

A 45-minute conference slot: approximately 25 minutes of narrative and visual encounters, 15 minutes of guided discussion and 5 minutes of questions. These are planning allocations; the full presentation has not been timed.

## Developed passages selected for main

The current published reading copy builds out the postcard opening, naming/taste passage and serious production objection. Meraki selected these from the developed draft and completed critique work; no new unanimous team vote is claimed.

The remaining route stays visible in this spine. Fuller performance development belongs on the workshop branch until a passage is deliberately promoted.

## Performance register

Understated humor in everyday invitation and personal taste; quiet, exact language at the production objection. Uppercase actor directions are silent cues. Two primary riff invitations: one personal taste example and one shared creative moment. Other riffs can be passed over.

The sock-sorting machine remains a footnote or informal setup aside. Its rendering has no slot in the five-work inventory.
