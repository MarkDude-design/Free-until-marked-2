## Proposal hook

Before deciding whether a picture is art, can we agree on what happened to make it?

## Long abstract (ready to paste)

Before deciding who deserves the name artist, what are we actually asking when we look at an image? This talk begins with a postcard: an invitation to a small encounter, not a demand that everyone share one judgment.

From there it separates questions too often bundled together: Is this art? Is it good? Is it for me? What was its production process? Whose labor or source material deserves recognition? Found images, a manual GIMP transformation of a pond photograph, and a carefully labeled set of generated comparisons make the distinctions visible.

The strongest objections to AI-assisted art—the source material, permission, credit, payment and effects on makers—get a serious hearing without requiring the audience to renounce personal taste. A practical open-source lesson survives the disagreement: record sources, manual edits, generated material and actual decisions in a readable, inspectable form. You can disagree about art while still being precise about what was made.

## Three takeaways

- Art-status, taste, quality and production are different questions.
- GIMP and source notes make the creative workflow inspectable.
- Credit and permissions require evidence, not visual guesses.
