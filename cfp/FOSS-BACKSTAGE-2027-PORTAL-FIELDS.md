## Abstract / teaser (223/350 characters)

What changes when an image is labeled AI-generated? A postcard, an edited pond photo and documented source notes help separate art-status, taste, production, permissions and credit—without telling the audience what to like.

## Description (946 characters; >=350 required)

A postcard is a small invitation: here is an image, and here is room for your response. In this talk I start with that simple exchange before revealing how a set of images came to be: a human photograph, a manual GIMP edit, found material and clearly marked generated alternatives.

The labels often become an argument before the underlying questions do. Is a work art? Is it good? Is it something I enjoy? Who made it? What was the source, and who gave permission to use it? Those are different questions, and a beautiful output cannot answer all of them.

For open source communities building or distributing creative tools, the practical takeaway is not an art verdict. It is a lightweight source and production ledger that distinguishes photographed input, human edit, generated output and the resulting credit/rights questions. The audience is welcome to disagree about taste while still asking for a readable account of how a work was made.
