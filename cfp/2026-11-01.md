## Title

Before the Name: Art, AI, and Attribution

## Short abstract

What changes when an image is labeled “AI-generated”—and what remains unanswered? Beginning with a postcard, this talk separates medium, art-status, quality, taste and production. Found material, a manually edited pond photograph and generated comparisons make room for enjoyment, attribution and serious objections while leaving the audience space to disagree.

## Long abstract

Before deciding who gets to be called an artist, what are we asking when we call something art?

Before the Name begins with a postcard: a small image offered to another person, who is free to respond. From that encounter, the talk separates several questions that are often bundled together: what kind of work is this, is it art, is it good, is it for me, and how was it produced?

The visual journey moves through found material, Monet and pond images, a manual GIMP transformation, a shared-making photograph, and three generated alternatives for an imaginary postcard. The performed version is planned around exactly five distinct generated visual works, identified in its source inventory. Human photography and the manual edit keep their own credits.

The strongest production objection gets a serious hearing: an appealing result does not settle questions about source material, permission, credit, payment or effects on other makers. The talk also leaves room for new makers to begin and for viewers to change—or retain—their judgments when source information becomes more visible.

For a FOSS audience, the practical thread is an inspectable workflow: distinguish the human source from the manual edit and generated work, record actual contributions, and maintain readable source notes alongside evolving images and slides. GIMP provides the manual-edit example; the talk materials are maintained in Markdown.

Participants leave with a small vocabulary for more precise art conversations and a way to discuss AI-assisted making without making taste carry every ethical or factual question.

The current spine, developed passages and evolving slide/media inventory are available at https://github.com/MarkDude-design/Free-until-marked-2.
