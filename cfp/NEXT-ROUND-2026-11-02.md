### Ready-to-paste abstract

What changes when a viewer learns how an image was produced? This talk starts with a postcard and separates questions that often collapse into one argument: Is this art? Is it good? Is it for me? How was it made? Who should receive credit?

A manually edited pond photograph in GIMP sits beside found material and a small set of explicitly marked generated comparisons. We use the visible steps and source notes to ask what a medium label can establish — and what it cannot. A pleasing result does not settle training-data consent, compensation, or credit, but neither does it erase the viewer's encounter or the maker's choices.

For open-source designers and user researchers, the practical output is a lightweight documentation habit: keep the human source, manual edits, generated works, rights/credits and presentation choices distinct and inspectable. Audience disagreement is allowed. A readable production record makes the disagreement more precise.

### Key takeaways

- Art-status, quality, taste and production are separate questions.
- GIMP edits, found material and generated media should carry distinct credits.
- A source note is more useful than arguing from appearance alone.

### Ready-to-paste alternate abstract

A postcard opens a conversation before anyone says 'AI'. We then disclose the steps: human photograph, manual GIMP intervention, found imagery and clearly labeled generated alternatives. Each disclosure changes what the audience is judging — the image, the process, the person, the permissions, or the distribution of credit. This accessible session offers FOSS communities a concrete source-labeling habit and an honest way to hear the strongest objections without dictating taste.
